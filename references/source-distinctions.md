# Source distinctions

Use this reference when checking attribution or revising the skill. Page numbers
refer to the printed pages in Harry G. Frankfurt, [*On Bullshit* (Princeton
University Press, 2005)](https://legacy.berea.edu/phi/wp-content/uploads/sites/81/2020/09/On-Bullshit.pdf).

| Passage | Distinction used in the design |
| --- | --- |
| pp. 6–19 | Black's self-presentation account is insufficient; intent is not readable from an utterance alone. |
| pp. 20–34 | Polish and intelligibility do not establish warrant. Frankfurt's reading of Wittgenstein's reaction to Pascal concerns unconstrained assertion, not an ordinary factual mistake; he acknowledges uncertainty about the anecdote. |
| pp. 35–43 | Open exploration differs from concealed pretense; irrelevance alone is insufficient. |
| pp. 45–56 | Authenticity and the represented enterprise matter independently of individual truth values. |
| pp. 63–67 | Pressure to pronounce beyond knowledge encourages bullshit; sincerity does not settle accuracy. |

Frankfurt supplies the conceptual inspiration, not a diagnostic taxonomy.
Pleonasm and quackery appear without operational procedures; nonsense and
gibberish also serve as contrasts. The unclarifiable is an added reminder here.

## Operational adaptations

The skill connects factual support with fidelity to the user's authentic intent
and expected outcome. This is a task criterion, not Frankfurt's definition of
truth. A true but irrelevant answer can fail that criterion; user agreement
cannot validate a false claim. A complaint starts an investigation without
establishing bullshit, a particular motive, or the user's preferred conclusion.

The **recovery enterprise** is this skill's bounded inquiry and repair workflow.
Its use of the term builds on comparing represented and actual activity; the
essay does not prescribe an agent recovery plan. Trace observable process
failures without claiming access to hidden motives. Correct ordinary errors too,
while preserving useful exploration, figurative language, and warranted claims.

The working record, claim and conduct checks, correction rules, and stopping
conditions are design choices for self-auditing. Evaluate whether they catch and
repair unsupported assertions, false process reports, and ineffective conduct,
while preserving warranted claims and necessary work. Pattern names serve as
reminders; distinguishing adjacent categories is not an evaluation objective.
The synthetic [evaluation cases](../evals/README.md) exercise those decisions;
they do not establish general effectiveness.
