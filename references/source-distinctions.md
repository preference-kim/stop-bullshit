# Source distinctions

Use this reference when checking attribution or revising the skill. Page numbers
refer to the printed pages in Harry G. Frankfurt, [*On Bullshit* (Princeton
University Press, 2005)](https://legacy.berea.edu/phi/wp-content/uploads/sites/81/2020/09/On-Bullshit.pdf).

| Passage | Distinction used in the design |
| --- | --- |
| pp. 6–19 | Black's self-presentation account is insufficient; intent is not readable from an utterance alone. |
| pp. 20–34 | Polish and intelligibility do not establish warrant; specificity can exceed knowledge. |
| pp. 35–43 | Open exploration differs from concealed pretense; irrelevance alone is insufficient. |
| pp. 45–56 | Authenticity and the represented enterprise matter independently of individual truth values. |
| pp. 63–67 | Pressure to pronounce beyond knowledge encourages bullshit; sincerity does not settle accuracy. |

Frankfurt supplies the inspiration, not the skill's scope or checklist. The
skill uses “bullshit” colloquially for sloppy, pointless, or unjustified agent
work, including conduct whose reporting may be factually accurate. A defect can
require correction without establishing Frankfurt's account of bullshit or an
agent's intention. Pleonasm and quackery appear without operational procedures;
the unclarifiable is an added reminder here.

The working record, claim and conduct checks, correction rules, and stopping
conditions are design choices for self-auditing. Evaluate whether they catch and
repair unsupported assertions, false process reports, and ineffective conduct,
while preserving warranted claims and necessary work. Pattern names serve as
reminders; distinguishing adjacent categories is not an evaluation objective.
The synthetic [evaluation cases](../evals/README.md) exercise those decisions;
they do not establish general effectiveness.
